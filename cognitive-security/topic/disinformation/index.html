Tensions & Open Questions

Both sides are retained. A tension is not an endorsement of either pole.

Canonical tension

Supply-Side Disruption vs Demand-Side Susceptibility

Should counter-influence efforts target the producers and infrastructure of harmful content or the demand-side vulnerabilities that persist when particular sources disappear?

Identify, attribute, and disrupt hostile actors and channels

Producers, infrastructure, finance, accounts, or channels are concentrated enough that disruption can reduce reach.

Change audience, platform, and community conditions that create traction

Replacement sources emerge while incentives, identity needs, and trust deficits continue to generate traction.

Supply disruption can create room for resilience, while resilience can lower returns to hostile supply.

Source: CT-017 · Supply-Side Disruption vs Demand-Side Susceptibility

Canonical tension

Credibility-Based Influence vs Falsehood and Saturation

Is long-term trustworthiness the decisive source of influence, or can speed, saturation, and manipulation create meaningful advantage despite weak credibility?

Build durable influence through truth, consistency, and authentic values

Consistency between words and actions, truthful reporting, and authentic values create compounding trust.

Generate effects through deception, volume, controlled channels, and cognitive exploitation

Channel control, low verification, repetition, and acute fear can enable short-term deceptive effects.

Credible actors still need reach, while deceptive systems often borrow credibility; these are organizing logics rather than pure types.

Source: CT-014 · Credibility-Based Influence vs Falsehood and Saturation

Canonical tension

Foreign Threat Focus vs Domestic Epistemic Vulnerability

Where should counter-influence attention concentrate when foreign actors exploit domestic conditions but consequential epistemic damage may also originate internally?

Prioritize hostile states, proxies, and transnational extremists

Foreign services, proxies, and extremists show intent and coordination warranting specialized resources.

Prioritize domestic actors, internal fractures, and homegrown vulnerability

Domestic polarization, distrust, identity conflict, and homegrown actors can cause equal or greater harm and enable foreign efforts.

Foreign actors exploit domestic vulnerabilities and domestic actors reuse foreign narratives; resources should follow mechanism and harm.

Source: CT-008 · Foreign Threat Focus vs Domestic Epistemic Vulnerability

Connections Across the Corpus

Explicitly selected themes and narratives, with the reason each is relevant here.

Cross-cutting theme

Epistemic Infrastructure for Contested Reality

Actors need durable capabilities to observe contested environments, verify sources and media, attribute activity, assess effects, and anticipate change while representing uncertainty honestly.

Why it is here: Verification and attribution make the source and reliability of claims inspectable.

Source: TH-02 · Epistemic Infrastructure for Contested Reality

Cross-cutting theme

Platform Architecture as the Terrain of Influence

Platforms, algorithms, moderation systems, media channels, access controls, and circumvention pathways shape what becomes visible, trusted, amplified, suppressed, or routed around.

Why it is here: Distribution and amplification shape the reach of deceptive content.

Source: TH-03 · Platform Architecture as the Terrain of Influence

Integrative narrative

Contested reality makes epistemic and platform infrastructure a strategic capability

Strategic advantage increasingly depends on observing, verifying, attributing, and interpreting information within platform architectures that shape visibility, access, and shared reality.

Why it is here: The broader narrative links contested reality to the systems that make information visible and verifiable.

Source: CN-02 · Contested reality makes epistemic and platform infrastructure a strategic capability
