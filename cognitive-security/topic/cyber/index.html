Tensions & Open Questions

Both sides are retained. A tension is not an endorsement of either pole.

Canonical tension

Supply-Side Disruption vs Demand-Side Susceptibility

Should counter-influence efforts target the producers and infrastructure of harmful content or the demand-side vulnerabilities that persist when particular sources disappear?

Identify, attribute, and disrupt hostile actors and channels

Producers, infrastructure, finance, accounts, or channels are concentrated enough that disruption can reduce reach.

Change audience, platform, and community conditions that create traction

Replacement sources emerge while incentives, identity needs, and trust deficits continue to generate traction.

Supply disruption can create room for resilience, while resilience can lower returns to hostile supply.

Source: CT-017 · Supply-Side Disruption vs Demand-Side Susceptibility

Canonical tension

Operational Speed and Optimization vs Legitimacy-Preserving Guardrails

How much risk and procedural compression can information operations accept before short-term tempo damages oversight, credibility, or public trust?

Compress sensing, approval, experimentation, and adjustment cycles

Adversary adaptation and fleeting opportunities impose costs on slow approval and sequential planning.

Preserve legal, ethical, procedural, and values-based controls

Hard-to-reverse influence makes compressed decisions externalize legal, ethical, and trust harms.

Preauthorization, red teams, circuit-breakers, logs, and escalation thresholds can create fast lanes inside safeguards.

Source: CT-019 · Operational Speed and Optimization vs Legitimacy-Preserving Guardrails

Connections Across the Corpus

Explicitly selected themes and narratives, with the reason each is relevant here.

Cross-cutting theme

Mission Continuity on a Contested Technical Substrate

Information-related activity depends on communications, data, sensing, positioning, cloud, cyber, electromagnetic, and platform infrastructures that must remain usable under disruption, denial, manipulation, and degradation.

Why it is here: Cyber activity depends on a technical substrate whose failure can disrupt missions and communities.

Source: TH-08 · Mission Continuity on a Contested Technical Substrate

Cross-cutting theme

Platform Architecture as the Terrain of Influence

Platforms, algorithms, moderation systems, media channels, access controls, and circumvention pathways shape what becomes visible, trusted, amplified, suppressed, or routed around.

Why it is here: Social cybersecurity and cyber-enabled influence connect technical access with the architecture of exposure.

Source: TH-03 · Platform Architecture as the Terrain of Influence
